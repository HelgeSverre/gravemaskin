# Research: Real-Time Soil & Terrain Simulation

> Produced by the gravemaskin research workflow, 2026-08-28.

## Summary

The strongest prior art for Gravemaskin's exact vision (solid volume -> loose particles -> re-settled volume) is the industrial training-sim lineage, not games: Holz et al. (CM Labs/Vortex, ISARC 2013) and Servin/Berglund/Nystedt (Algoryx agxTerrain, AMSES 2021). Both implement precisely the three-representation pipeline at real-time rates on CPU. Servin's multiscale model stores resting soil as a regular 3D grid (0.1 m voxels) plus a 2D heightfield for the free surface; when a tool contacts soil it predicts a wedge-shaped "active zone" using the Rankine failure angle extended to sloped terrain, converts that solid mass to a modest number of variable-size rigid particles (~950 in their excavation benchmark), aggregates them into a single low-dimensional rigid body with frictional-cohesive contacts against the bucket, and converts particles that come to rest outside the active zone back into grid mass with a swell/compaction factor. It runs at 16.7 ms steps (60 Hz), real-time factor 1.5, agrees with full DEM to 10-25%, and is ~1500x faster. Holz gives the complementary force model: the Fundamental Equation of Earthmoving (Reece 1964/McKyes 1985) discretized into parallel 2D wedge slices across the tool width, with a narrow-band SDF (clamped to +/-one cell) tracking the soil surface, CSG boolean subtraction of the tool's swept volume producing the removed volume dV that spawns surcharge particles. This is directly portable to F#/BEPU2 at 60 Hz on CPU.

Shipped games confirm the practical engineering envelope. MudRunner: 2.5D heightfield + mud-layer heightfield, 16 m blocks, simulation only "active" near the vehicle, physics on CPU at low resolution and rendering data GPU-resident. Astroneer: density-voxel chunks + marching cubes repolygonization of only touched chunks. Deep Rock Galactic: CSG mesh subtraction, hardness values instead of physics, disconnected terrain simply vanishes. Teardown: everything is voxel volumes with CPU voxel-vs-voxel collision. Construction Simulator: heightmap deformation clamped inside invisible per-mission volumes with spoil piles conserving volume. None of them do real soil mechanics; the training sims do, which is the gap this game can occupy. On meshing: Burst/SIMD marching cubes and surface nets both hit 0.1-0.5 ms per 32^3 chunk single-threaded on old hardware, so incremental CPU remeshing of a handful of dirty chunks per frame is comfortably within a 60 Hz budget in .NET; MPM is confirmed GPU-only for real-time (and macOS OpenGL 4.1 has no compute shaders anyway), and large-count PBD/DEM particle systems are unnecessary because the AGX result shows ~1000 rigid-body clumps suffice for convincing excavator soil.

## Key findings

- Servin et al. 2021 (agxTerrain, arXiv:2011.00459 / AMSES 8:2021) is the definitive prior art for the exact 3-state pipeline: solid soil = regular 3D grid (voxel size 0.1 m, cell volume l0^3) + 2D heightfield for the free surface; active zone = wedge predicted with failure angle theta = pi/2 - (phi+beta)/2 (Rankine extended to slope), discretized into parallel lateral wedges; disturbed soil = variable-size rigid particles (size range [d_min,d_max], capped growth rate); particles aggregate into a single rigid body with the particles' mass/momentum and frictional-cohesive contacts constraining the machine's multibody dynamics; particles at rest outside the active zone convert back to grid mass, raising the heightfield.
- Servin performance: excavation benchmark ran 8 rigid bodies + 950 particles, 85 direct-solver equations + 1.8e4 iterative equations, 10 PGS iterations, 16.7 ms timestep (60 Hz), real-time factor 1.5 — vs reference DEM (50k particles, 500 iterations, 1 ms step) at real-time factor 0.001. Force/displacement accuracy within 10-25% of DEM. Mass conservation is exact 'to machine precision' by construction: removed volume dV is fully assigned to created particles; resettled particles get compaction w = 1/(1+s) via swell factor S = rho_bank/rho_loose.
- Servin's soil parameter table (bank-state bulk params usable directly for game materials): gravel phi=44deg c=0 dilatancy=11deg E=4.6MPa; dry sand phi=39deg c=0 psi=9deg; wet sand phi=33deg c=8.7kPa psi=8deg; dirt phi=40deg c=2.1kPa psi=13deg; weak clay-like soil phi=21deg c=4.8kPa psi=6deg. Cohesion (kPa-scale) is the clay-vs-sand knob; it also raises the standable slope: stability criterion is slope beta < phi for cohesionless soil, with cohesion adding safety margin ~ c/(gamma*z*sin(beta)*cos(beta)) so cohesive walls stand vertically up to a critical depth — this single term gives trench-wall-collapse gameplay for free.
- Holz et al. ISARC 2013 (CM Labs Vortex) gives the complete real-time bucket force recipe: FEE cutting force per unit width F = gamma*d^2*N_gamma + c*d*N_c + Q*N_Q + c_a*d*N_a, with closed-form N-factors from trial-wedge static equilibrium (N_gamma = (cot(rho)+cot(beta))sin(alpha+phi+beta)/(2 sin(delta+rho+phi+beta)) etc., inputs: soil slope alpha, tool/soil angle rho, penetration depth d, failure angle beta, internal friction phi, cohesion c, unit weight gamma, tool-soil friction delta, adhesion c_a, surcharge Q). Tool is discretized into parallel slicing planes along the tool width axis; each slice runs the 2D FEE and forces are summed to a total force + moment applied as a 6-DOF constraint. Known FEE singularity when delta+rho+phi+beta >= pi (F and R collinear -> infinite predicted force): detect and substitute a tuned high resistance scaled linearly with depth.
- Holz terrain representation: signed distance function sampled on a uniform 3D grid, narrow-band clamped to [-dx, +dx] (bandwidth = one cell) — sufficient because only the gradient is needed for visualization; per-vertex soil volume approximated as V(p) = (-f(p)/dx)*dx^3 for f<0. Tool carving = discrete CSG boolean: f_new(p) = max(f_soil(p), -f_tool(p)) evaluated only near the tool; removed volume per vertex dV(p) = difference between steps, deposited as surcharge particles on the failing wedge whose measured contact forces feed back into FEE as the surcharge term Q. Velocity-direction sign flips of friction/adhesion smoothed with delta' = tanh(-c1*v_bw.i_b)*delta to avoid oscillation. Terrain slope per slice estimated by tracing the SDF zero-crossing cells (marching-cubes-style edge tests) and fitting a weighted least-squares line with exponentially decaying weights away from the tool.
- MPM is not viable on CPU at game framerates: a SIMD-vectorized CPU MPM is >100x slower than single-GPU implementations; the one real-time MPM demo (Fei et al. 2021) needed four Tesla V100s for 55.9 FPS. PBD granular claims ~500k particles at interactive rates on high-end CPU (Interactive High-Resolution Simulation of Granular Material, 2023), but this is unnecessary: the AGX result proves ~1000 rigid clump particles + an aggregate body is enough for excavator-grade soil. macOS OpenGL 4.1 has no compute shaders, so all GPU-compute routes (GPU MPM, GPU falling-sand block CA, GPU DEM) are closed on the primary platform anyway.
- Meshing perf (C#/Burst reference points, first-gen Ryzen 1700 single thread): fastMarchingCubes ~0.2-0.5 ms per 32x32x32 chunk with SIMD (32 cubes at a time via cornermask, normals in fragment shader); fastNaiveSurfaceNets ~0.1-0.4 ms per 32^3 with fully shared vertices; naive Burst-jobbed MC ~0.85 ms per 16^3. GPU MC on RTX 2070: 2 ms for 64^3. Greedy meshing is only relevant for blocky Minecraft-style voxels — for SDF/density terrain the choice is marching cubes vs surface nets/dual contouring; surface nets gives smaller meshes and shared vertices and is simpler than dual contouring (no QEF solve, no sharp features needed for dirt).
- Voxel storage practice: vertical-column RLE gives ~65x compression on real terrain (1 GB -> 16 MB) and RLE+LZ4 600-1200x (Roblox: 64 KB chunk -> 256 bytes best case; zeux.io production writeup); random access into naive run lists is O(runs), so keep hot chunks as flat arrays and RLE only for storage/undisturbed chunks. Space Engineers uses compressed octree storage (MyOctreeStorage) holding player edits as deltas over a procedural provider, LOD0 authored and higher LODs derived. MudRunner: 1x1 km world in 16x16 m blocks, per-block mud heightfield, physics only for blocks near the vehicle, packed A8R8G8B8 textures (25x25 base, 128x128 mud) as GPU-resident render data.
- Angle-of-repose settling is a solved heightfield cellular automaton: for each column compare height to 4 or 8 neighbors; if dh > tan(phi_repose)*cell_spacing, transfer material downhill; iterate until stable. Race-condition-free parallelization uses block cellular automata (Margolus 2x2 neighborhood alternating offsets), which also makes matter conservation trivial. A University of Regina thesis ('A Voxel-Based Approach to the Real-Time Simulation of Sands and Soils') uses a multi-level heightfield over voxels for slope stability and slippage — the right pattern for overhang-capable terrain: run the CA on surface column heights extracted from the volume, only in dirty regions.
- BEPUphysics v2 has NO heightfield/terrain collidable (v1 Terrain was removed): official guidance is 'just Mesh' via CreateDeformedPlane-style construction; v2 Mesh uses a SAH-sweep-built binary tree, so deforming terrain carries acceleration-structure maintenance cost. The practical pattern is per-chunk Static Mesh objects rebuilt (or Tree-refit) only when a chunk's surface mesh changes, keeping broadphase pollution low (one broadphase entry per chunk regardless of triangle count). Dev-forum consensus: a dedicated terrain type would mainly save construction time/memory, not query time — so chunked meshes are fine.
- Shipped-game techniques survey: Deep Rock Galactic = CSG boolean subtraction on meshes in UE4, per-material integer hardness (1-3 pickaxe hits), zero structural simulation (any connection keeps a pillar standing; fully disconnected pieces instantly crumble to dust) — proof that aggressive gameplay simplification reads fine. Astroneer = signed density voxels + marching cubes, deformation = modify densities + repolygonize only the touched chunks; known pitfall: duplicated chunk-edge data updated inconsistently causes cracks. Teardown = thousands of independent voxel volumes, CPU voxel-vs-voxel collision, ~0.1 m voxels, continuous structural-integrity recalculation. Construction Simulator = heightmap digging clamped inside invisible mission-authored 3D pit volumes, displaced material piles up elsewhere (volume-conserving), terrain snapped to target states between mission phases.
- Force-model alternatives ladder found in the literature: (1) pure FEE with static wedge (Reece/McKyes) — cheap, 2D, valid for wide tools and for buckets because side walls suppress lateral flow (Shen & Kushwaha); (2) Holz per-slice discretized FEE with velocity effects and surcharge particles — the training-sim standard; (3) Servin aggregate-body formulation — FEE extended to dynamic conditions incl. bucket penetration resistance and exterior-surface contacts, best coupling quality with a rigid-body engine; (4) DEM/FEM — hours per simulated second, offline only. Recent autonomous-excavation work (ETH, arXiv 2506.22579, 2309.02575) calibrates FEE soil parameters online from measured bucket forces, confirming FEE remains the accuracy/cost sweet spot in 2025-26.

## Recommendations

- Adopt the Servin/agxTerrain architecture wholesale as the soil pipeline spec: chunked solid volume + cached surface heightfield -> active-zone wedge prediction on tool contact -> convert wedge mass to BEPU2 sphere rigid bodies (variable radius clumps, target <1500 live particles, hard cap with merge-on-overflow) -> settle-and-convert back to volume with swell factor. Their 60 Hz / RTF 1.5 numbers were achieved on ordinary CPUs with a full nonsmooth multibody solver; BEPU2 is faster than that solver class, so the budget closes.
- Terrain data structure: 32x32x32 chunks of cell structs stored as struct-of-arrays (material id: byte, density/occupancy: byte or ushort fraction, moisture: byte, compaction: byte), Y-up column-major within a chunk so vertical runs are cache-contiguous; SDF/density value per cell drives meshing (narrow band clamped to +/- one cell per Holz — do NOT maintain a true global SDF, it buys nothing). Keep hot (near-machine) chunks as flat arrays; RLE-compress cold/undisturbed chunks (vertical-column RLE, ~65x on real terrain) and for save files add LZ4/Deflate on top. Use NativeMemory/GC.AllocateUninitializedArray + ArrayPool to keep this off the GC heap; F# structs with [<Struct>] and Span-based access.
- Maintain a 2D surface height cache (one float column height per XZ cell, derived from the volume, updated only for dirty columns). Run ALL of: angle-of-repose settling CA, tool slope estimation, and particle deposition against this cache; touch the 3D volume only when the tool carves overhangs/vertical trench faces. This is exactly the Servin split (3D grid + 2D free-surface heightmap) and keeps the per-frame CA cost proportional to disturbed area, MudRunner-style (only simulate blocks near the machine).
- Bucket-soil forces: implement Holz's discretized FEE. Slice the bucket cutting edge into ~8-16 slices along the width axis; per slice compute penetration depth d and local soil slope alpha by tracing the surface cache (weighted least-squares line, weights decaying away from the tool), then F = gamma*d^2*N_gamma + c*d*N_c + Q*N_Q + c_a*d*N_a with the closed-form N-factors; sum to force+torque and apply to the bucket body in BEPU2 each 60 Hz step (either as external force or as a one-body constraint for stiffness). Must-implement details from the paper: singularity guard (delta+rho+phi+beta >= pi -> substitute tuned depth-scaled resistance), tanh velocity smoothing for friction/adhesion sign (delta' = tanh(-c1*v.i_b)*delta), and surcharge Q measured from the weight of loose particles resting on the failure wedge.
- Mass conservation contract (make it an invariant with an assert in the sim): every cell carries an occupancy fraction; carving computes dV exactly from occupancy deltas; spawned particles are created with total volume == dV / (1) and flagged with bank density; settling writes particle volume * 1/(1+swell) back as loose-density cells; compaction (driving over loose soil) increases density toward bank without changing mass. Track one f64 total-mass accumulator per material and assert conservation each step in debug builds — the Servin paper proves this is achievable to machine precision.
- Angle of repose: heightfield CA on the surface cache. Per material, repose angle = internal friction angle phi (sand 34-39deg, gravel 44deg, wet sand 33deg but add cohesion, dirt 40deg, clay ~21deg + cohesion). Cohesion term: allow a column to exceed repose slope while the local wall height z < h_crit(c, gamma) (h_crit ~ 4c/gamma classical Taylor estimate); when exceeded (or when vibrated by tracks / hit by bucket), fail the whole face at once by converting the over-steep wedge to particles — this gives dramatic, correct trench-wall collapse rather than gradual ooze. Run the CA as a Margolus block CA (checkerboard 2x2 alternating offsets) over dirty regions only, budgeted (e.g. max N column updates/frame, carry dirty set over frames).
- Meshing: use Surface Nets (not greedy meshing — that is for blocky voxels; not full dual contouring — no sharp features needed in dirt) over the density field, per 32^3 chunk, on worker threads, with a completed-mesh queue applied on the main thread. Expected cost ~0.2-1 ms per chunk in optimized .NET (Burst reference is 0.1-0.5 ms; plain .NET with System.Numerics/Vector<T> will be within 2-4x; digging touches only 1-4 chunks per swing). Share one-cell borders between chunks (sample neighbor data, don't duplicate-and-desync) to avoid Astroneer's crack bug. Reuse Ironsight's Renderer.fs/GlUtil.fs chunk-VBO update pattern; orphan or persistent-map VBOs, OpenGL 4.1-safe.
- BEPU2 integration: one Static with a Mesh per terrain chunk (broadphase sees one object per chunk); when a chunk remeshes, dispose and re-add its Static (or swap the Mesh shape) — at 32^2-quad scale the SAH tree build is sub-millisecond and only dirty chunks pay it. Consider colliding the machine against the surface heightfield analytically (custom one-body contacts for tracks: sample 4-8 points per track under the surface cache, Bekker-style sinkage force) and reserve triangle-mesh collision for buried rocks and non-soil geometry — this sidesteps mesh-rebuild churn under the moving machine entirely. Loose soil particles = BEPU2 spheres with per-material friction, plus a weak cohesion via short-range attractive constraint only for clay-type materials.
- Follow the training-sim trick for bucket fill: soil inside the bucket should stop being particles ASAP — when particle velocity relative to bucket falls below threshold and it is inside the bucket volume, convert it to 'bucket load' scalar mass/volume (affects machine COM and hydraulics load directly), re-emitting particles only on dump. This is where most of the particle budget is saved and it makes the load-in-bucket affect tipping physics exactly as the vision requires.
- Read order for implementation: (1) Servin et al. AMSES 2021 (the architecture), (2) Holz ISARC 2013 PDF already saved locally at /Users/helge/.claude/projects/-Users-helge-code-gravemaskin/376b6ec1-08db-425d-99a1-9e169d31de46/tool-results/webfetch-1787948488787-h2lfjq.pdf (the force math, eqs 1-6 and Figure 2), (3) zeux.io voxel storage post (chunk storage engineering), (4) Gamedeveloper.com MudRunner writeup (activation/LOD discipline for a 1 km world at 60 Hz).

## Risks

- OpenGL 4.1 ceiling on macOS closes every GPU-compute path (GPU MPM, GPU falling-sand CA, GPU DEM, compute-shader meshing). The whole soil sim AND meshing must fit the CPU frame budget; transform feedback / fragment-shader tricks exist but are not worth the complexity. Budget realistically: ~2-3 ms soil (CA + particle conversion + FEE) + ~1-2 ms meshing amortized + BEPU2 step, inside 16.7 ms alongside rendering.
- Particle count explosion: a big greedy bucket pass through loose soil can spawn far more clumps than the ~1000-particle envelope. Mitigations are mandatory, not optional: max spawn rate, radius growth (merge small clumps into bigger spheres, AGX does variable-size for exactly this), aggressive settle-to-grid timeouts, and bucket-load scalar conversion. Without them the 60 Hz fixed step will blow up on the first trench.
- BEPU2 static churn: naively rebuilding terrain-chunk Statics every frame while tracks deform soil underneath will thrash the broadphase and generate contact discontinuities (machine 'pops'). Prefer analytic track-vs-heightfield contacts; if using mesh Statics, only rebuild when net height change exceeds a threshold and never rebuild the chunk directly under a resting contact in the same step it is touched.
- Determinism: the fixed 60 Hz functional-core pattern needs BEPU2 determinism — BEPU2 is deterministic only for same binary + same platform (and multithreaded dispatch order can vary unless the thread dispatcher is deterministic). If replay/lockstep matters, pin thread count and use deterministic dispatch; floating-point across ARM (Apple Silicon) vs x64 will NOT match.
- Mass leaks are insidious: SDF/density carving with clamped narrow bands does not conserve volume by itself (Holz's V(p) mapping is an approximation). Conservation must come from explicit occupancy accounting (Servin-style), not from integrating the SDF. Skipping the debug-mode mass assert will cost weeks later.
- FEE edge cases: the singularity (infinite predicted force) WILL be hit in normal play (steep rake angles, curling the bucket); the wedge model also assumes tool velocity roughly parallel to the slicing plane — pure vertical stabs and twisting motions need the fallback resistance path. Expect significant hand-tuning of c1, nominal depth, and the singular-resistance scale; treat them as calibration knobs in the material table, not constants.
- 2.5D/3D split complexity: the surface-heightfield cache is the workhorse, but vertical trench faces and overhangs break the one-height-per-column assumption. Either accept a multi-level heightfield (spans) for the cache, or restrict the repose CA to the topmost surface and let rare undercuts collapse via the cohesion-failure path. Deciding this early avoids rewriting the settling system.
- Chunk seams: duplicated border cells that get updated on one side only produce cracks (Astroneer's documented bug) and physics/visual mismatch. Sample neighbor chunks during meshing instead of duplicating data, and mark neighbor chunks dirty when a border cell changes.
- .NET-specific perf cliffs: GC pauses from per-frame allocations (mesh buffers, particle lists) will break the fixed step — pool everything; JIT auto-vectorization is weaker than Burst, so the 0.2-0.5 ms/chunk Burst numbers may be 1-2 ms in idiomatic F# — still fine, but write the mesher against flat arrays with Span, avoid F# list/seq in the hot path.
- Scope risk: the training-sim papers model far more than the game needs (dilatancy, elasticity, Bekker sinkage). DRG's lesson is that players accept huge simplifications (no structural integrity at all) when the core loop feels right. Implement FEE forces + repose CA + mass conservation first; moisture, compaction layering, and track sinkage are additive later.
