# Research: Excavator Mechanics & Hydraulic Simulation

> Produced by the gravemaskin research workflow, 2026-08-28.

## Summary

Excavator simulation for a game decomposes cleanly into four coupled subsystems, and the research strongly supports a specific architecture: (1) a rigid-body linkage (swing yaw joint, boom/stick/bucket pitch joints) driven by motorized constraints whose max-force and max-velocity limits are derived from real hydraulic data (force = relief pressure x piston area, velocity = pump flow / piston area); (2) a quasistatic hydraulic layer that never simulates fluid, only a flow budget — pump flow is split across active functions each tick, so operating three functions simultaneously slows each one, and pressure saturates at the relief cap — this is exactly how the academically documented simplified models (arXiv 2102.11381 nonsmooth quasistatic actuators) and training-sim vendors structure it; (3) soil-tool coupling via the Servin/Algoryx "aggregate body" method: predict the failure-zone soil mass from Reece's fundamental equation of earthmoving, wrap it in one temporary rigid body per bucket, and couple it to the bucket with frictional-cohesive contacts — this reproduces digging resistance to 10-25% of DEM at >1000x speed and makes stalling, straining, and dragging-the-machine-with-the-bucket emerge for free from the rigid-body solver; (4) tracks as a few contact boxes per side with a conveyor-belt surface-velocity trick and tuned anisotropic friction, not per-shoe simulation.

Concrete tuning numbers were extracted from primary spec sheets. Cat 320 (20t class, from the official Caterpillar technical spec PDF): 21,700 kg operating weight, boom 5.7 m, stick 2.9 m, bucket 1.19 m³ with 1.57 m tip radius, bucket digging force 150 kN / stick 106 kN (163/115 kN with boost), boom cylinders 2x 120 mm bore x 1260 mm stroke, stick cylinder 140x1504 mm, bucket cylinder 120x1104 mm, pumps 429 L/min total at 35 MPa (38 MPa boost), swing 11.25 rpm at 82 kN·m max torque (27.5 MPa swing circuit), travel 5.7 km/h with 205 kN drawbar, ground pressure ~45 kPa, counterweight 4200 kg, tail swing 2.83 m, track gauge 2.38 m x length 4.45 m. Kubota U17 (1.7t mini, from workshop manual + RitchieSpecs): 1730 kg, 11.2 kW, dig depth 2.31 m, reach 3.84 m, bucket breakout ~15.2 kN / arm ~8.5 kN, pumps 2x17.3 + 10.4 L/min at 21.6/18.6 MPa relief, swing 9.1 rpm, travel 2.25/4.25 km/h, ground pressure ~25.5 kPa. Component masses for the 320 (undercarriage frame 4390 kg, track shoes 2690 kg/side, counterweight 4200 kg, boom 1900 kg, stick+bucket-cylinder 1110 kg, bucket 940 kg, swing frame 1910 kg) give the mass distribution needed for emergent tipping; ISO 10567 defines rated lift as min(87% hydraulic capacity, 75% tipping load), a ready-made sanity check for the sim.

## Key findings

- LINKAGE TOPOLOGY: An excavator arm is a serial chain (swing bearing yaw -> boom pivot pitch -> stick pivot pitch -> bucket pivot pitch) where each hydraulic cylinder closes a triangle across a joint; cylinder force converts to joint torque as tau = F_cyl x d, where d = perpendicular distance from joint pivot to the cylinder's line of action, which varies through the stroke (this is why curl force peaks mid-stroke). The bucket is NOT directly driven: the bucket cylinder pushes a 'dog-bone' (idler) link pinned to the stick plus a 'power/H-link' pinned to the bucket, forming a 4-bar that converts ~1.1 m of cylinder stroke into ~170-190 degrees of bucket rotation and multiplies curl force near the dig position. The dogbone-angle -> bucket-angle mapping is a law-of-cosines triangle decomposition across the linkage diagonal FG (fully worked in Trimble patents US10017914/US10900196): FG^2 = DF^2 + DG^2 - 2*DF*DG*cos(180 - theta_DF).
- CAT 320 GEOMETRY (official Cat spec PDF AEXQ3461): boom 5.7 m (1900 kg incl. lines/pins/stick cylinder), stick R2.9B1 = 2.9 m (1110 kg incl. bucket cylinder + linkage), alt stick 2.5 m; bucket 1.19 m³ HD = 940 kg, tip radius 1570 mm. Max dig depth 6720 mm, max reach at ground 9860 mm, max cutting height 9370 mm, max loading height 6490 mm, max vertical wall depth 5190 mm.
- CAT 320 CYLINDERS (from same PDF): boom = 2 cylinders, 120 mm bore x 1260 mm stroke (pair weighs 340 kg); stick = 1 cylinder 140 mm bore x 1504 mm stroke; bucket = 1 cylinder 120 mm bore x 1104 mm stroke. At 35 MPa: one 120 mm cylinder gives 396 kN extend (piston area 0.0113 m²); retract force is lower by the rod area (rod ~85 mm on this class -> annulus ~0.0057 m², ~200 kN) — this extend/retract asymmetry is real and audible in gameplay (boom-down is weaker than boom-up... actually boom-up uses extend; bucket dump weaker than curl).
- CAT 320 FORCES/RATES: bucket digging force (ISO 6015) 150 kN, stick digging force 106 kN; with Auto Dig Boost (38 MPa) 163/115 kN. Swing speed 11.25 rpm (1.18 rad/s), max swing torque 82 kN·m, swing circuit relief 27.5 MPa (lower than implement relief — swing has its own cap). Travel max 5.7 km/h (auto two-speed), max drawbar pull 205 kN (≈ machine weight x ~0.96 — tracks can generate nearly 1 g of tractive force on firm ground), travel circuit relief 34.3 MPa. Gradeability 35 degrees / 70%.
- CAT 320 HYDRAULIC BUDGET: two pumps, 215 L/min each = 429 L/min total implement flow, main relief 35,000 kPa (38,000 kPa heavy-lift/boost). Engine Cat C4.4, 129.4 kW at 2000 rpm. Hydraulic power ceiling ≈ 35e6 Pa x 429/60000 m³/s ≈ 250 kW theoretical, engine-limited to ~120 kW usable — meaning the pump can deliver full flow OR full pressure, not both; a corner-power (P*Q <= engine power) clamp is a realistic and cheap model.
- CAT 320 MASS DISTRIBUTION (Major Component Weights table — directly usable for BEPU body masses): base machine w/o boom or counterweight 13,930-14,960 kg depending on config; counterweight 3700/4200/4700 kg options; undercarriage base frame 4390 kg; track shoe sets 2690 kg per side (600 mm triple grouser, 49 shoes/side); swing frame 1910 kg; boom 1900 kg; stick 1110 kg; bucket 890-970 kg; boom cylinder pair 340 kg; fuel+operator 310 kg. Roughly: undercarriage ~35%, house+counterweight ~45%, front linkage ~20% of the 21,700 kg total. Track gauge 2380 mm, track length 4450 mm, length to roller centers 3650 mm (this is the true support polygon length), tail swing radius 2830 mm, ground clearance 470 mm, cab height 2960 mm.
- KUBOTA U17 (1.7t mini): operating weight ~1730 kg (3703 lb with canopy; ~1815 kg with operator), engine Kubota D902 11.2 kW (15 hp), bucket 0.04 m³ standard. Dig depth 2.31 m, max reach 3.84 m (12'6"), dump height ~2.44 m, dig height ~3.53 m. Bucket breakout ~15.2 kN (3417 lbf), arm crowd force ~8.5 kN (1918 lbf). Swing 9.1 rpm, travel 2.25 km/h low / 4.25 km/h high (1.4/2.6 mph), ground pressure ~25.5 kPa (3.7 psi), hydraulically variable track gauge 990-1240 mm, 230 mm rubber tracks. Boom ~1.9 m and arm ~0.93-1.10 m are typical for the class (exact segment lengths not published; scale from dig-depth/reach envelope). Minis also have boom swing (offset) and a dozer blade — two extra functions a 20t machine lacks.
- KUBOTA U17 HYDRAULICS (workshop manual): three gear pump sections P1/P2 at 16.6-17.3 L/min each relieving at 21.6 MPa, P3 at 10.4 L/min relieving at 18.6 MPa. Total ~45 L/min. This 3-circuit split is why a mini feels different from a 20t: functions are hard-assigned to circuits, and two functions on the same circuit visibly halve each other's speed.
- HYDRAULIC MODEL FOR GAMES (arXiv 2102.11381 'Nonsmooth Quasistatic Modeling of Hydraulic Actuators' is the key reference): skip fluid compressibility entirely; model each function as a motorized constraint with (a) max force = relief_pressure x piston_area (different extend vs retract), (b) target velocity = Q_allocated / piston_area, (c) flow balance at a junction: Q_pump = sum(Q_actuators) + Q_relief. When total demand exceeds pump flow, scale allocations (flow-sharing/pressure-compensated behavior) — this single rule produces the authentic 'three functions at once are slower' feel. System pressure = highest active load pressure (load-sensing); when any function hits relief its velocity drops to whatever the load permits (stall), while others keep moving. CM Labs Vortex does exactly this layered thing: pump layer -> divider manifolds -> combiner manifolds -> actuators, plus a low-pass filter on actuator commands to fake spool/pilot inertia.
- JOYSTICK->VALVE RESPONSE: real pilot systems have 10-20% stick deadband (spool land overlap), then a progressive metering curve (valve area vs spool stroke shaped by spool notches — start shallow for feathering, steepen to full flow), plus hysteresis. Operators can feather loads because flow (hence cylinder velocity) is proportional to valve opening and largely independent of load until relief pressure — i.e., it is a VELOCITY control, not a force control. Game model: deadband -> x^1.5-x^2 curve on stick input -> first-order lag (tau ~ 80-150 ms) -> target velocity for the constraint motor. One patent notes ~14 of 30 degrees of real lever travel can be eaten by deadband — don't copy that, but keep ~10%.
- ISO vs SAE CONTROL PATTERNS (ISO 10968 / SAE J1177 vs SAE J1814): both patterns share left-stick-X = swing (left/right) and right-stick-X = bucket (left = curl in, right = dump). ISO ('CAT controls', worldwide default): left Y = stick/arm (fwd = arm away/out, back = arm in/close); right Y = boom (fwd = boom down, back = boom up). SAE ('John Deere/backhoe controls'): swaps boom and stick between hands — left Y = boom (fwd down, back up), right Y = stick (fwd away, back close). Travel is two independent levers/pedals, one per track, fwd = that track forward (enables pivot and counter-rotation). Ship both patterns with a toggle, ISO default.
- SOIL-TOOL FORCE MODEL — Reece's Fundamental Equation of Earthmoving (1964): F = gamma*d²*w*N_gamma + c*d*w*N_c + Q*N_q + c_a*d*w*N_a, where gamma = soil unit weight, d = cutting depth, w = tool width, c = cohesion, c_a = tool-soil adhesion, Q = surcharge (soil piled above the cut), N-factors from failure-wedge geometry (functions of internal friction angle phi, tool-soil friction delta, rake angle; planar failure surface is an accepted approximation of the log-spiral). CMU's Luengo/Singh/Cannon reformulation extends it to sloped terrain and bucket geometry with online soil-parameter estimation — this is the standard game/robotics resistance model.
- AGX/ALGORYX AGGREGATE-BODY COUPLING (Servin, Berglund, Nystedt, 'A multiscale model of terrain dynamics for real-time earthmoving simulation', AMSES 2021 / arXiv 2011.00459 — the definitive paper for this project): terrain is a 3D grid of cells (mass, compaction, soil type) with a 2D heightfield surface; a 'shovel' converts solid cells in the predicted failure zone into dynamic mass; that active mass is aggregated into ONE rigid body per digging tool with proper mass/momentum/frictional-cohesive contact properties, and THAT body is what pushes back on the bucket through the ordinary rigid-body solver. Result: digging resistance within 10-25% of high-res DEM reference, >1000x faster, and machine strain/stall/tip-over emerge naturally because the resistance enters as ordinary constraint forces. Soil particles (6-DOF spheres with Hertz elasticity, Coulomb friction, rolling resistance) are only used for the visible loose mass in/around the bucket. AGX groups material params into bulk (density, friction angle, cohesion, dilatancy), compaction, particle, and excavation-contact categories, with presets for gravel/sand/dirt.
- TRACKS FOR GAMES ('Fast Simulation of Vehicles with Non-deformable Tracks', Pecka et al., arXiv 1703.04316, is the best single reference): consensus is rigid hull + simplified colliders (a few boxes/spheres or raycasts per track), tracks rendered visually only. The Contact Surface Motion / 'conveyor belt' trick: set the contact surface velocity of the track collider to the commanded track speed each step (in BEPU2: override the target velocity in the contact friction constraint, or apply equivalent tractive forces at contact points capped by mu*N) — propulsion, skid steering, pivot turns, and counter-rotation then all emerge from friction. Skid-steer slip can be approximated kinematically via Instantaneous Centers of Rotation (Martinez et al.) if the emergent version misbehaves. Lateral friction must be handled carefully: pure anisotropic friction (high longitudinal grip, lower lateral) is what makes skid steering feel right.
- GROUND PRESSURE / SOFT SOIL: nominal ground pressure = weight / (2 x track_length_on_ground x shoe_width): Cat 320 ~44-46 kPa, U17 ~25.5 kPa. Sinkage on soft soil via Bekker/Wong pressure-sinkage: p = (k_c/b + k_phi) * z^n (b = shoe width, z = sinkage, n~1 for loam); the SCM (Soil Contact Model) is the standard real-time generalization. For the game: compute per-contact-box normal load, derive sinkage from the cell's compaction/moisture, add motion resistance and reduced tractive mu when sunk — connects tracks to the voxel soil pipeline directly.
- TIPPING: with correct component masses (see 320 table), tipping needs NO special code — it emerges from rigid-body dynamics when the net moment about a support-polygon edge (front/rear roller line at +-1825 mm from center, track outer edges at +-1490 mm) exceeds zero. The polygon is length-to-roller-centers (3650 mm) x track gauge + shoe width (2980 mm outer), NOT full track length — the sprocket/idler overhangs carry little load. ISO 10567 rated lift = min(87% of hydraulic capacity, 75% of tipping load) — use as a validation target: the sim's measured tipping load over the side should be ~1.33x the published rated capacity. Digging force > machine anchoring is real: a 320's 150 kN breakout vs 21.7t weight means curling the bucket against hard ground WILL lift the front of the tracks (moment arm ~5-9 m vs counterweight at ~-3 m) — the classic 'lift yourself out of the hole' behavior falls out free.
- FORCE FEEDBACK LOOP (why the machine strains): apply soil resistance as contact/constraint forces on the bucket body; the solver propagates them through bucket->stick->boom joints; each cylinder constraint motor has finite max force, so when required force exceeds P_relief x A the joint stops advancing (stall) while the input still commands velocity — no special-case code. If the bucket is hooked into unbroken soil (SDF still solid), treat it as a high-force contact against the static volume: the tracks will then slide or the house will pivot, letting the player drag the machine with the bucket, exactly as real operators do.
- SWING DYNAMICS TUNING: Cat 320 house+arm yaw inertia is roughly 1.5-2.5 x 10^5 kg·m² (arm extended); 82 kN·m accelerates that to 11.25 rpm (1.18 rad/s) in roughly 0.3-0.5 s including hydraulic ramp — real machines feel 'heavy then coasting'; swing circuit has its own relief (27.5 MPa) and freewheels slightly on stop (anti-rebound valves), so model swing as a velocity-target motor with torque cap ~80 kN·m (20t) / ~4 kN·m (1.7t est.) and a small brake torque when centered. Mini swing: 9.1 rpm, much lower inertia.
- TRAINING-SIM PRECEDENT: CM Labs Vortex excavator uses hydrostatic vehicle-systems model with rigid tracks, low-pass-filtered hydraulic actuator commands, layered pump/manifold/actuator hydraulic graph, and hybrid heightfield->particle soil (heightfield converts to DEM particles when tool force crosses a threshold); a 2025 arXiv paper (2505.19330) documents Vortex's FEE-for-sloped-soil configuration. Algoryx AGX uses the aggregate multiscale model above. Both vendors confirm the game-viable pattern: no fluid simulation, constraint-motor actuators, analytic soil failure forces, particles only for visible loose mass.

## Recommendations

- Model each hydraulic function as a BEPU2 motorized constraint (AngularAxisMotor / LinearAxisMotor on the cylinder's prismatic representation) with per-direction MaximumForce = relief_pressure x (piston_area | annulus_area) and TargetVelocity = allocated_flow / area. BEPU2's SpringSettings give you the 'stiff constraint' stand-in for fluid stiffness — do not simulate compressibility.
- Write the hydraulic layer as a pure F# function per fixed step: HydraulicsState -> stick inputs -> flow allocation. Flow balance: demand_i = |input_i| x Q_max_i; if sum(demand) > Q_pump then scale all by Q_pump/sum (flow sharing). Add the engine corner-power clamp: sum(P_i x Q_i) <= engine_power x efficiency (~0.85). Two numbers per machine: U17 = 45 L/min @ 21.6 MPa; Cat 320 = 429 L/min @ 35 MPa (38 boost).
- Represent the linkage as 4 rigid bodies (house, boom, stick, bucket) + revolute joints; compute cylinder geometry (anchor points, current length, moment arm) in F# each step and drive the revolute joints with torque = F_cyl x moment_arm rather than simulating cylinder bodies — fewer bodies, no closed-loop constraint headaches in BEPU2. Model the bucket 4-bar analytically (law-of-cosines mapping from the Trimble patents) to get bucket angle and effective curl-force multiplier from cylinder stroke; don't put dog-bone/H-link in the physics.
- Adopt the Algoryx aggregate-body pattern for digging: each step the bucket is cutting, (1) compute failure wedge from cut depth d, width w, rake angle via Reece FEE using the soil cells' gamma/c/phi; (2) either apply the FEE force directly to the bucket at the cutting edge as an external force+torque (cheapest, start here) or create one temporary kinematic-mass aggregate body coupled by contacts (upgrade for better feel); (3) move cell mass into the bucket's load state. Loose soil particles are cosmetic + settling only — never the force path.
- Input pipeline per stick axis: deadband 10% -> curve x^1.7 -> first-order lag (tau = 100 ms) -> target velocity. Ship ISO and SAE patterns (exact mappings in findings) plus per-track travel controls; gamepad default: left/right stick = ISO, triggers/bumpers or dpad = tracks.
- Tracks: 3-4 static-friction contact boxes per side along the roller path (length 3650 mm for 320, ~1250 mm for U17), conveyor-belt surface velocity = commanded track speed, anisotropic friction (mu_long ~0.9, mu_lat ~0.6 on firm ground; scale both down with soil moisture/looseness). Cap tractive force at mu x N per box so drawbar ~205 kN (320) emerges. Add Bekker sinkage only after the voxel soil pipeline exists — hook it to cell compaction.
- Set component masses from the Cat 320 table (scale for the mini): house 9500 kg + counterweight 4200 kg (COM ~1.5-2 m behind swing axis), undercarriage 8000 kg (COM low, ~0.5 m), boom 1900, stick 1110, bucket 940 + up to ~2000 kg soil load. Tipping, self-lifting on the bucket, and slope behavior then need zero special code. Validate: side tipping load should be ~1.33x ISO 10567 rated capacity.
- Start with the U17 as the first playable machine: smaller forces, three-circuit hydraulics give the most audible flow-sharing feedback, boom-swing + blade add gameplay variety, and its 15 kN breakout vs 17 kN weight means digging visibly rocks the machine — great feel per engineering hour.
- Tuning table starting values (mini / 20t): relief 21.6 / 35 MPa; pump 45 / 429 L/min; bucket breakout 15 / 150 kN; arm crowd 8.5 / 106 kN; swing 9.1 / 11.25 rpm; swing torque ~4 / 82 kN·m; travel 4.25 / 5.7 km/h; drawbar ~18 / 205 kN; ground pressure 25 / 45 kPa; mass 1730 / 21,700 kg; reach 3.84 / 9.86 m; dig depth 2.31 / 6.72 m.
- Read arXiv 2011.00459 (Servin multiscale terrain) in full before designing the soil pipeline — it is effectively the blueprint for the three-representation soil vision (solid grid -> particles -> aggregate coupling -> settle back), validated numbers included.

## Risks

- Driving cylinders as literal BEPU2 rigid bodies in closed loops (boom + cylinder + house triangle) creates constraint cycles that iterative solvers handle poorly — expect jitter/explosion at 60 Hz with heavy masses and 400 kN forces. Use analytic cylinder->joint-torque conversion instead; only fall back to real prismatic bodies if you need cylinder collision.
- Mass ratios are extreme: 21,700 kg machine vs 940 kg bucket vs ~50 kg soil clumps. BEPU2 (like all iterative solvers) loses stiffness at high mass ratios; keep soil particles out of direct constraint contact with the heavy bodies (aggregate them), raise solver iterations for the machine island, and consider substepping (BEPU2 supports it) for the linkage.
- Applying raw FEE forces without smoothing causes force pops at cell boundaries and when the cut depth d jumps — low-pass the resistance force and ramp the failure-zone recompute; otherwise the arm buzzes.
- The conveyor-belt track trick interacts badly with deformable terrain if contact points penetrate the changing heightfield/SDF — recompute track contact against the CURRENT surface each step, and clamp sinkage rate, or the machine will fall through freshly dug cells.
- Flow-sharing model without the engine power clamp lets players get full force AND full speed simultaneously — machines then feel weightless. Conversely, forgetting extend/retract area asymmetry makes boom-down as strong as boom-up and dump as strong as curl, which operators will notice immediately.
- Swing on its own relief (27.5 vs 35 MPa on the 320): if you give swing the implement torque cap, the house whips and the game feels arcadey; if you forget swing brake/anti-rebound, the house oscillates on every stop.
- Ground pressure/soft-soil sinkage is a rabbit hole: full Bekker per-contact terramechanics can eat the frame budget; ship rigid ground + friction scaling first, add sinkage as a compaction-cell lookup later.
- Spec-sheet numbers vary by revision (swing torque 74 vs 82 kN·m, weight 21.7 vs 22.5 t across regional Cat 320 sheets; U17 pump 16.6 vs 17.3 L/min across manual sections) — pick one config per machine and freeze it in a data file; don't chase reconciling sources.
- Published 'breakout force' (ISO 6015) is measured at a specific geometry (bucket tip, max moment arm) — it is a validation target, not a constant to apply; your force at the tip must come from cylinder force x current linkage leverage, and should only MATCH 150 kN at the reference pose.
- macOS OpenGL 4.1 has no compute shaders — any plan to offload soil particle physics to GPU compute is dead on arrival on the primary platform; keep soil physics CPU-side (or Metal-via-veldrid later), which reinforces choosing the aggregate model over per-particle DEM forces.
